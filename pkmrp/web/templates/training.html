How to use PK-MRP

PK-MRP plans purchasing from the orders customers have actually placed. It tells you what to buy today, why, and what it's holding up — and it never changes anything in MAX. Ten minutes here and you'll know your way around.

It's in shadow mode

For now PK-MRP is watching, not driving. It records what it would recommend and, later, what was actually bought, so we can see whether its advice is better. Keep buying the way you do today; use it as a second opinion. When a group of parts is switched to "live", buyers act on it directly — you'll see that here first.

Your morning, in five steps

  1. Open Today. The top 10 buys are ranked by the booked orders each one is holding up. Start at number 1.
  2. Open "Why this buy?" on anything that surprises you. It says when the part runs out, how many to order and from whom, when it would land, and which orders slip.
  3. Place it with everything else from that vendor. Click the vendor's name on the card ("+3 more to order with it"): the vendor's whole order opens — what's due now plus what comes due in the next few weeks — with their part numbers, ticked and totalled. Untick what shouldn't go, then Copy for the PO or download the CSV.
  4. Pick who's on it (Chad / Rob / Aaron) from the dropdown on the card, so nobody doubles up. Mark done when the PO is in — add the PO number if you have it. Everyone sees it. On the next plan PK-MRP checks MAX: if the buy is gone it's confirmed; if MAX still needs it, the card comes back marked no PO in MAX. Every Done is listed on the Done page, kept for good.
  5. Check Overbuys once a week. It lists open POs nobody needs and what was placed in the last 30 days beyond need — the cheapest money we'll ever save.

The pages

Today
This week's buys, top 10 first, and what came off the list since the last run. Buyers, every morning.
All buys
Everything to place within five working days; filter by who's on it (or nobody yet), or search by part or description. Buyers.
By vendor
Who do we need to order from, and what all goes on it? Every vendor with something to buy in the next 4 weeks, those with buys due now first. Each opens one order: due-now and late lines plus what's coming up (choose 1 to 12 weeks), with their part numbers, pack counts, totals, recent open POs with that vendor, and Copy / CSV for keying the PO. Buyers, whenever placing a PO.
Analytics
Four questions. Is PK-MRP saving money and orders? — where what buyers did matched its advice (POs placed by the order-by date, push-outs and cancels done), the orders and dollars behind them, and the money found on Overbuys. In shadow mode a match isn't proof PK-MRP caused it. Is the plan getting healthier? — buys due, late, and orders waiting, day by day. Where is today's lateness? — by how late, product and vendor. Which vendors deliver? — on-time % against the date first asked, and real lead time vs MAX's. Jared, Chad and supply chain, weekly.
Promise
Type a part, a quantity and the date a customer wants: the earliest date material supports, and the part that decides it. Material only — build capacity isn't in yet. Order Admin, before dating an order.
Committed
Which parts can't wait for an order? The few whose real lead time is longer than orders give warning of get a 12-month plan: requirement, buffer, a proposed blanket order with monthly releases, and a forecast letter for the supplier. It's a buying page. Buyers and supply chain, monthly.
Pipeline
Which MAX part is each sales SKU? Book/Bill 2.0 forecasts in sales names ("2300AG-B", "FK11-SAE-U"), so each SKU is matched to a MAX part once, by a person, before its forecast can feed Committed. PK-MRP proposes the match from the part number, the description and past orders, surest first; you click Use this, pick another part, or mark it not material. Bookings with no billing month are listed for fixing in Book/Bill. Whoever has an admin role in PK-MRP, as new SKUs appear.
Lead times
Which numbers in MAX are wrong? Where MAX's stored lead time is more than 25% off what the part really takes, with a proposed value and a CSV to key in. It's a data clean-up page — nothing to buy. Whoever maintains part data, quarterly.
Fix in MAX
Everything in MAX still naming a part an ECN obsoleted: order lines (Order Admin), forecast entries (planning), released work orders and live BOMs (Engineering, through an ECN), plus order lines on parts an open ECN will obsolete. "Change to" is the part the same ECN released, or a replacement already set in PK-MRP. Download the CSV for a bulk change. Rows drop off by themselves once MAX is corrected. Order Admin and planning, weekly; Tim for bulk changes.
Sourcing ideas
Where would buying differently save money or build hours? Switch vendor (cheaper, and not worse on on-time % or returns — a gap over 2.5x is marked check scope, usually raw vs finished or bare vs assembled), quoted price breaks, build-it-here (the hours that still break even at $171/h), buy-it-instead (and quote long builds when hours are the constraint), vendors to negotiate with, catalog prices from DigiKey/Mouser/Octopart once keys are in, and standard costs to fix (CSV). Dollars are a year at a year's quantity. Buyers and Chad, weekly.
EOL watch
Which parts are makers ending, and what should we buy before they go? From distributor lifecycle status, PCN/EOL emails in the purchasing mailbox (read by Claude) and a weekly maker news search, matched to our parts by manufacturer part number. Shows where each is used, booked demand, cover and a last-time-buy quantity (booked + 2 years, less stock and POs). Engineering obsoletes are on Fix in MAX instead. A part newly found obsolete, end of life, last-time buy or NRND is emailed once to Jared and Patrick. Buyers, Engineering, Jared.
Vendor prices
Are vendors raising prices on us, and where? The same parts this year against last year — overall, per vendor and per part — from every PO receipt, per unit (packs converted), weighted by last year's quantities so volume and mix don't move it. Drill from a vendor to a part to every PO, open or received, with standard cost, PPV and invoice. The "Look at these" list flags price jumps, open POs over standard, parts cheaper from another vendor, vendor-wide increases and likely pack/each slips, biggest dollars first; mark each Reviewed with a note. (Mark's dashboard chains month to month instead; on lot-size-driven prices that drifts, so the numbers differ.) Jared, Chad, buyers — weekly.
Weekly email
Fridays at 3 pm: the week in one page — plan movement, what was placed and confirmed in MAX, advice followed, next week's top five, housekeeping. Preview it, send yourself a test, and (admins) set who gets it. Jared, supply chain, leadership.
Feedback
The feedback button (bottom right) sends a note or screenshot straight to PK Feedback — anything wrong, confusing or missing. Everyone.
Replacements
When Engineering replaces a part but orders, work orders or BOMs in MAX still name the old one, a planner sets old → new on the Notes page. From the next plan, the demand moves to the new part (and through its BOM), and the old part shows a → new part chip. Tick use up when the old stock can still be used. The orders in MAX still need correcting; this just keeps the buying right meanwhile. Planners / Jared.
The quantity on Today
One sensible order, not the bare minimum. A part shows up when something is due within 5 working days, and the quantity covers booked orders for a stretch of weeks sized to the part: cheap, steady parts cover more (a $36 cable used about 10 a week covers about 9 weeks), expensive parts cover only what's needed now, and long-lead committed parts follow their blanket plan. The card says how far it covers ("covers orders through 22 Jan") and how many can't wait. Why this buy? explains the stretch. If a PO for the part went in during the last two weeks, the card names it — add to that PO instead of cutting a second one.
Decision check
Runs after every hourly plan and catches wasted work: parts wanted again right after a PO, the same part on repeat POs, buys that changed sharply since the hour before (the card gets a red changed chip — check before ordering), buys that came and went with no PO, and late buys left sitting. Each row says what to do. Today shows a one-line count when there's something to look at. Buyers and Jared, weekly.
ECN chips
Read every plan from Ron's ECN app, the source of truth for engineering changes. ECN 126168 · In Process means Engineering is changing the part — check before buying a lot. obsolete · ECN 126056 means the part's latest closed ECN set it to rev ZZ (PK's mark for obsolete; MAX has no obsolete flag). Click a chip to see the part's ECNs in the ECN app. Buys of obsoleted parts are listed at the top of Today. Buyers before placing; Engineering to clear the cause.
Notes
What people know about a part that MAX doesn't say — a replacement coming from Engineering, obsolete, an ECN being created, on hold, a supplier problem. Add one with + note next to any part number; it shows as a chip next to that part on every page until someone removes it. Notes don't change the plan — except Hold — don't buy yet, which takes the part off every buy list (Today, All buys, By vendor, shelf carts) until the note is removed; held parts are listed under On hold on Today. Everyone — Engineering, buyers, planners.
Overbuys
Open POs nobody needs, POs bought for more than will be needed, POs arriving early, and stock with no order behind it — in dollars. When one is legitimate (spares, at a CM, minimum buy, already fixed with the vendor), Clear… it with the reason: it leaves the list and the totals, and comes back only if it grows. Buyers and supply chain, weekly.

Every list has a search box: type part of a number (dashes optional) or words from the description, in any order — "relay lens", "2300 z stage", "fpga".

How it decides

  • The backlog is the plan. Everyday parts are bought only against orders customers have placed, never against a forecast. That's what stops overbuying.
  • A few parts are committed. When a part's own history shows it takes longer than orders give warning of (over about 90 days), it gets a 12-month plan instead. There are about 120 of these.
  • It walks the bill of materials. An order for a system becomes demand for every assembly and part inside it, timed by how long each level takes to build.
  • Stock and open orders come first. It only proposes a buy for what stock and open POs and WOs don't cover.
  • Kits we supply to vendors count. For boards a contract manufacturer builds, it plans the parts we send them — learned from what's actually been sent.
  • Order size is worked out, not lot-for-lot. Each order covers booked demand for the number of weeks that balances the cost of placing and receiving a PO (about $75 a line) against carrying the stock (25% a year): T = √(2 × $75 ÷ (25% × yearly use × unit cost)). It never orders beyond booked orders, never less than what can't wait, and stops at 26 weeks or $15K over the minimum.
  • Lead times come from history. The median and slow end of each part's real POs, not just MAX's setting.

Words you'll see

Order by
The last day to place a PO and still have it arrive in time.
Covered / to buy / slips
For an order waiting on a part: stock or an open PO covers it; a buy placed today lands in time; or it's needed before any new buy could arrive.
Committed
A long-lead part bought on a 12-month plan, not order by order.
Unproven long-lead
MAX says it takes a long time, but there's no history yet to prove it. Checked, not committed.
Working days (wd)
Monday to Friday — the unit MAX keeps lead times in. 5 working days ≈ 1 week; 63 ≈ 13 weeks ≈ 90 calendar days.
P80 / slow end
80% of past orders arrived within this time. Used where being late is costly.
Beyond the backlog
An open PO on a long-lead part that no booked order needs yet — bought ahead on purpose, not a mistake.
Frozen / firm / indicative
On a supplier forecast: months 1–3, 4–6 and 7–12.
Needs a source
A part booked orders need that has never been on a PO and has no approved vendor in MAX. Its lead time and price are MAX's placeholders, so it isn't ranked — it's quoted.
Shelf part
A part of $50 or less from a catalog distributor — or from any vendor whose orders usually arrive within 6 working days (Webstaurant, Amazon Business, Zoro…). It's planned on that distributor's real delivery time (McMaster: about 2 working days) and ordered in the Shelf order, not ranked.
Packs
Quantities are in the unit MAX stocks (usually each). Where MAX buys in packs, reels or boxes, the buy is rounded up to whole packs and says so: "100 to buy (1 PK of 100)". Key the PO in MAX in each, as always; the Shelf order's paste uses pack counts, which is what the catalog sells.
Sales SKU / catch-all
The name Book/Bill 2.0 sells a thing under. A catch-all like "2304 Option" stands for any option of that model — the booking's line text says which — so it maps to no one part.
Due now / coming up
On a vendor order: "due now" must be placed within 5 working days; "coming up" isn't late yet but falls in the window you chose, so it can ride on the same PO.
Shadow mode
PK-MRP records its advice and compares it with what was done, but nobody is asked to follow it yet.

"Why isn't my part on the list?"

  • Stock or an open PO already covers it. Search it on All buys or check the order on Promise.
  • The order is parked on a placeholder date (2030 or later). Those lines stay off the plan until they get a real date.
  • The part has no BOM, or it's typed bulk (Y) in MAX. Neither can be planned until the part data is fixed — the home page counts them under "Kept off the plan".
  • It's a long-lead part. Look on Committed instead — those are planned a year out.
  • It's a shelf part — something cheap a catalog distributor or online store (McMaster, Digi-Key, Webstaurant, Amazon…) ships in days. Those are in the Shelf order box on Today, one cart per vendor, with a button that copies their part numbers for a quick order.
  • Nobody has bought it before and MAX has no vendor for it. It's under Needs a source on Today — quote it, set the vendor up in MAX, and it joins the ranked list.
  • It's due later than this week. All buys shows what to place in the next five working days; later buys come up when their order-by date gets close.
  • It was just ordered for several weeks. A part placed at the recommended quantity stays off the list until the orders after that stretch come due.
